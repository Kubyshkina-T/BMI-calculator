Underweight

A Body Mass Index (BMI) below 18.5 is considered an indicator of underweight. This means that body weight in relation to height may be lower than optimal for the normal functioning of the body.

When body weight is below the recommended range, the body may lack sufficient energy and nutrients to maintain proper metabolism, hormonal balance, and immune function. For some individuals, a low BMI is related to natural body composition. However, in certain cases, it may result from chronic stress, insufficient nutrition, or increased physical demands.

It is important to consider that prolonged underweight can be associated with reduced endurance, fatigue, and slower recovery after physical activity. Therefore, if your BMI falls below the normal range, attention should be given not only to body weight but also to overall well-being.

If BMI indicates underweight, the primary goal should be a gradual and balanced recovery: adequate nutrition, sufficient intake of proteins, fats, and carbohydrates, moderate physical activity, and quality sleep. In some cases, consulting a healthcare professional may be helpful to determine the underlying causes of low body weight.

In addition to improving overall nutrition, it may be helpful to increase calorie intake gradually by choosing nutrient-dense foods such as whole grains, healthy fats, dairy products, nuts, seeds, and lean proteins. The goal is to support the body with quality energy rather than simply increasing portion sizes.

Monitoring overall health, including energy levels, sleep quality, and emotional well-being, is equally important. If symptoms such as persistent fatigue, dizziness, or hormonal irregularities occur, seeking medical advice is strongly recommended.
